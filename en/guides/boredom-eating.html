Boredom Eating: Why We Snack Without Hunger and What Helps

Updated November 26, 2026 · ToughLove editorial team · about 5 min read

In short: Boredom is one of the most common reasons for eating without hunger. Food quickly adds a bit of stimulation and distraction to an empty moment. If you know your idle times, pause briefly and fill the moment differently, you’ll snack much less often, without any bans.

Why boredom makes you hungry

Boredom feels unpleasant: your mind looks for stimulation, anything to make the moment more interesting. Food delivers that instantly: taste, crunch, a small reward. In research on an emotional eating scale, boredom was a distinct trigger alongside anger, anxiety and sadness (Koball et al., 2012). Researchers in Ireland also found indications that people eat to escape the unpleasant feeling of boredom, and tend to choose unhealthier food (Moynihan et al., 2015).

What a lab study showed

In an experiment, participants watched either a very boring or a neutral film with chocolate sweets within reach. Bored people ate more of them. Even more surprising: in a second study, bored people even gave themselves mild electric shocks more often, just so something would happen (Havermans et al., 2015). Boredom eating is about stimulation, not hunger.

Boredom hunger or real hunger?

  • Real hunger builds slowly, your stomach speaks up and almost any food would do.
  • Boredom hunger comes suddenly, often shortly after a meal, and wants something specific, usually sweet, salty or crunchy.
  • It shows up in idle moments: in front of the TV, while scrolling, in the office in the afternoon, while waiting.
  • As soon as you’re occupied, it’s often simply gone.

The honest question: “Would I eat an apple right now?” If not, it’s probably not hunger.

7 things that help against boredom eating

  1. Find your idle times. For a few days, note when you eat without hunger. It’s usually the same two or three situations, and you can plan for them.
  2. Ten minutes between urge and eating. You don’t have to say no, just “in a moment”. Often the urge has passed by then.
  3. Keep your hands busy. Fetch a glass of water, tidy something, water the plants. Boredom wants activity, not necessarily calories.
  4. Feed your mind, not your stomach. A short call, a podcast, a puzzle: something that really stimulates.
  5. Leave the situation. Five minutes of fresh air or a walk round the block. A change of place interrupts the habit.
  6. Move snacks out of sight. What isn’t on your desk or coffee table gets eaten less on the side.
  7. Eat proper regular meals. If meals fill you up, you have less reason to go “searching” in between. When you eat, do it mindfully, not from the packet.

Frequently asked questions

How can I tell if I’m eating out of boredom?

Boredom hunger comes suddenly, often shortly after a meal and in idle moments such as in front of the TV or at your desk. Real hunger builds slowly and almost any food satisfies it.

What helps right away against boredom eating?

Pause and ask: am I hungry or just under-stimulated? Then spend ten minutes on something that keeps your hands or mind busy, such as getting up, messaging someone or stepping outside.

Is it bad to eat out of boredom?

Occasionally it’s completely normal. It becomes a problem if it happens daily and you feel bad afterwards. Then it’s worth finding your typical idle moments and filling them on purpose.
