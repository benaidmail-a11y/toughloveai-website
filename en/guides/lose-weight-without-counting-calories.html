Can You Lose Weight Without Counting Calories?

Updated November 26, 2026 · ToughLove editorial team · about 5 min read

In short: Yes, for many people it works. Energy balance still applies, but you don’t have to track it in numbers. Studies show that people also lose weight without calorie targets when they focus on filling, minimally processed foods and better habits.

Why calorie counting often fails

  • It’s inaccurate. Portions are estimated, nutrition values vary and hardly anyone counts the oil in the pan.
  • It’s exhausting. Few people keep weighing and logging every meal for more than a few weeks.
  • It turns food into maths. For some people counting becomes compulsive. If you’re prone to eating disorders, it can even be harmful.
  • It doesn’t change the why. If you eat out of stress or boredom, a calorie app won’t change that.

What the research shows

In the large DIETFITS study, more than 600 adults got no calorie targets. Instead they were asked to eat high-quality, minimally processed food with plenty of vegetables, either lower in fat or lower in carbohydrates. After twelve months, both groups had lost around 5 to 6 kilograms on average (Gardner et al., 2018). What mattered wasn’t a number in an app but food quality and changed habits.

5 principles instead of counting

  1. The plate method. Half vegetables or salad, a quarter protein (fish, meat, eggs, tofu, legumes), a quarter sides (potatoes, whole grains, rice). No weighing needed.
  2. Filling foods first. Protein and fibre keep you full longer. Start with them and you’ll naturally eat less of the rest.
  3. Eat slowly and notice fullness. Put your cutlery down now and then. Stop when you’re comfortably full, not when the plate is empty.
  4. Don’t forget drinks. Soft drinks, juices and sweet coffee drinks barely fill you up but carry lots of energy. Water, tea and coffee without sugar are the easiest lever.
  5. Work on habits, not willpower. One small change a week is enough. In one study, it took a median of 66 days for a new habit to become automatic, with a wide range of 18 to 254 days (Lally et al., 2010). Patience is part of the plan.

Track progress without fixating on the scale

Your weight shifts by one to two kilos a day depending on water and digestion. More telling questions are: how often did you stress eat this week? How do your trousers fit? On how many days did you stick to your plan? If you only watch the scale, you may give up at the first fluctuation.

When calorie counting can still make sense

For some people, counting for a short time helps them get a feel for portions. With certain conditions, such as diabetes, your diet should be agreed with a doctor or dietitian anyway.

Frequently asked questions

Can you lose weight without counting calories?

Yes. Studies such as DIETFITS show that people also lose weight without calorie targets when they focus on minimally processed, filling foods and better habits.

How long does it take to form a new habit?

In one study, a median of 66 days, with a range of 18 to 254 days. Small steps and patience matter more than perfection.

What is the plate method?

Half the plate vegetables, a quarter protein, a quarter sides. That gives you a balanced meal without weighing or counting.
