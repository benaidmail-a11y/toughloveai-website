Lack of Sleep and Cravings: Why Sleep Helps Weight Loss

Updated November 26, 2026 · ToughLove editorial team · about 5 min read

In short: Just a few short nights raise the hunger hormone ghrelin, lower the satiety hormone leptin and make you crave sweet and fatty food. In studies, people ate about 385 more kilocalories per day on average after shortened sleep. Good sleep is one of the most underrated aids for weight loss.

What lack of sleep does to your hunger

In a classic study, young men slept only four hours for two nights. Afterwards the hunger hormone ghrelin was 28% higher, the satiety hormone leptin 18% lower, and hunger rose clearly, especially for carb-rich foods like sweets and bread (Spiegel et al., 2004).

The brain reacts too: after a sleepless night, areas that drive reward and craving were more active, while areas for judgement and control were less so. High-calorie foods seemed more desirable to participants (Greer, Goldstein & Walker, 2013).

How much more people eat

A review of several studies showed that after shortened sleep, people consumed about 385 more kilocalories per day on average, without burning more energy (Al Khatib et al., 2017). That’s roughly an extra small meal, day after day.

Signs that sleep is your issue

  • After short nights, you crave sweets already in the morning.
  • In the evening it’s especially hard to stop snacking.
  • You need coffee and sugar to get through the day.
  • At weekends you sleep much longer than during the week.

6 tips for better sleep

  1. Regular times. Go to bed and get up at similar times every day, weekends included.
  2. Put your phone away. No screens for the last half hour. Charge it out of reach.
  3. Stop caffeine early. Ideally have coffee, energy drinks and black tea only until early afternoon.
  4. Avoid heavy late meals. A big meal right before bed can disturb sleep.
  5. Get daylight. A few minutes outside in the morning helps your body clock.
  6. An evening ritual. Tea, reading, breathing exercises: a routine tells your body it’s bedtime, and replaces TV snacking along the way.

If you sleep badly for a long time despite good habits, snore loudly or are very tired during the day, get it checked by a doctor.

Frequently asked questions

Does lack of sleep make you gain weight?

It changes hunger and satiety hormones and increases appetite. In studies, people ate about 385 more kilocalories per day on average after shortened sleep. Over time this can favour weight gain.

Why do I crave sweets after little sleep?

After short nights, the hunger hormone ghrelin rises, the satiety hormone leptin falls and the brain’s reward system reacts more strongly to high-calorie food.

How much sleep do I need to lose weight?

Seven to nine hours a night is recommended for most adults. Regular sleep times matter almost as much as duration.
