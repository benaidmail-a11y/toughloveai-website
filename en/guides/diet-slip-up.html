Diet Slip-Ups: Why “Now It Doesn’t Matter” Is the Real Problem

Updated November 26, 2026 · ToughLove editorial team · about 5 min read

In short: It isn’t the slip itself that does harm but what comes after: “The day’s ruined anyway.” Researchers have observed this effect since the 1970s, especially in people who tightly control their eating. What helps: no all-or-nothing rules, being kind to yourself and simply having a normal next meal.

The “what the hell” effect

In a classic experiment, participants first drank one or two milkshakes or nothing, and could then taste as much ice cream as they liked. People who didn’t usually watch their food ate less ice cream after the shakes, which makes sense because they were full. People who strongly restrained their eating ate more after the shakes (Herman & Mack, 1975). The rule was broken, so “it doesn’t matter now”.

Many people know this: a slice of cake in the afternoon turns into a bag of crisps at night because the day is “ruined anyway”. Then comes the restart “on Monday”.

Why all-or-nothing rules are risky

Strict bans like “never chocolate again” or “no bread after 6 pm” feel good at first. The catch: they only know “success” or “failure”. A single slip tips everything over. Flexible guidelines like “I enjoy sweets mindfully, mostly at weekends” can survive a weak moment.

Kindness beats strictness

Blaming yourself after a slip feels like taking responsibility, but it makes the next overeating more likely. In one study, participants who were encouraged to be self-compassionate after a “forbidden” sweet ate less afterwards than those who weren’t (Adams & Leary, 2007).

How to carry on after a slip

  1. Describe, don’t judge. “I ate more than I wanted to.” Not: “I’ve messed it up again.”
  2. The next meal is normal. Don’t skip it, don’t “make up” for it. Just continue as planned.
  3. No “starting Monday”. The best time to restart is your next decision.
  4. Briefly ask why. Tired? Stressed? Went too long without food? You learn more from that than from any calorie.
  5. Talk to a friend instead of the fridge. Said out loud, a slip loses its drama.

A slip is a data point, not a verdict. Progress shows over weeks, not in one day.

Frequently asked questions

Is my diet ruined after a slip-up?

No. A single slip has hardly any effect on your weight. It only becomes a problem when it turns into “now it doesn’t matter” and the rest of the day or week tips over.

Should I eat less after a binge?

No. Skipping meals or “making up” for it makes the next craving more likely. Better eat your next meal normally. If binges happen often, get medical or therapeutic support.

What is the what-the-hell effect?

It’s what researchers call eating even more after breaking a strict food rule because the day already seems lost. Flexible rules and self-compassion weaken the effect.
