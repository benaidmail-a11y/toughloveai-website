Portion Size: Why We Eat What’s on the Plate

Updated November 26, 2026 · ToughLove editorial team · about 5 min read

In short: We eat less according to hunger than according to what’s in front of us. In studies, people ate about a third more from a portion twice as large and didn’t feel fuller afterwards. If you shape your portions deliberately and eat without distraction, you naturally eat more appropriately, no counting needed.

What studies on portion size show

In an experiment, adults were served different sizes of pasta bake on different days and could eat as much as they liked. With the largest portion they ate about 30% more than with the smallest, yet rated their hunger and fullness the same afterwards (Rolls, Morris & Roe, 2002). Most didn’t even notice the difference.

A review of many studies confirms the pattern: when the portion is doubled, the amount eaten rises on average by more than a third (Zlatevska, Dubelaar & Holden, 2014).

Distraction makes it worse

People who eat in front of the TV, on their phone or at their desk often eat more, especially later in the day, because the memory of the meal blurs. Conversely, eating attentively helped in studies (Robinson et al., 2013).

7 ways to get portions right without counting

  1. Serve on a plate. Not from the packet or the pot. That way you see how much it is.
  2. The plate rule. Half vegetables or salad, a quarter protein (fish, meat, legumes, eggs), a quarter sides.
  3. Take one serving first. Seconds are allowed, but only after a short break.
  4. Put leftovers away at once. What isn’t on the table won’t get “quickly” eaten.
  5. Portion your snacks. A handful in a bowl, the packet back in the cupboard.
  6. Share or take away at restaurants. A huge portion is not an order.
  7. Eat without screens. At least for one meal a day.

Your hand instead of a scale

A simple rule of thumb: a portion of protein is about the size of your palm, a portion of sides like your fist, and vegetables can happily fill two hands. It isn’t exact, and it doesn’t have to be. It’s about a good sense of quantity, not numbers.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I always clear my plate?

Most people go more by the portion than by hunger. Studies show that people eat clearly more from larger portions without feeling fuller afterwards.

How big should a portion be?

A simple guide: half a plate of vegetables, a quarter protein, a quarter sides. Your palm roughly shows a portion of protein, your fist a portion of sides.

Does it help not to watch TV while eating?

Yes. Distracted eaters often eat more, especially later in the day. Attentive eating helps you sense fullness better.
