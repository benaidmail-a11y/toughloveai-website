How Often Should You Weigh Yourself When Losing Weight?

Updated November 26, 2026 · ToughLove editorial team · about 5 min read

In short: In many studies, regular weighing is linked to more weight loss success, but only if you treat the number as a measurement, not a verdict about you. Weight fluctuates day to day, especially after weekends and holidays. What counts is the trend over weeks, not a single morning.

What research says about weighing

A systematic review analysed many studies on self-weighing: people who weigh themselves regularly tend to lose more weight on average and keep it off better, and most studies found no sign that regular weighing harms adults psychologically (Zheng et al., 2015). The reason is simple: the scale gives early feedback. If you see where things are heading, you can react before two kilos become ten.

But an average isn’t a person. If the number ruins your day every morning, frequent weighing is the wrong tool for you.

Why the scale says something different every day

Your body weight isn’t a fixed value. Water, salt, gut contents, your cycle and exercise can quickly shift it by one or two kilos without any change in body fat. In an analysis of daily weigh-ins, weight was highest after the weekend and fell during the week (Orsama et al., 2014). A European study found the same weekly patterns, plus increases around holidays such as Christmas (Turicchi et al., 2020).

For you: a gain on Monday is normal and no proof that “nothing works”.

Daily, weekly or not at all?

  • Daily suits you if you stay calm about fluctuations and look at the weekly average rather than single readings.
  • Once a week suits you if daily swings make you nervous. Then always use the same weekday.
  • Not at all is perfectly fine if the scale stresses you a lot. There are other ways to see progress.

5 rules so the scale helps rather than harms

  1. Always the same conditions. In the morning, after the toilet, before breakfast, same scale, similar clothing.
  2. Trend over daily value. Record readings and compare weekly averages, not Tuesday with Wednesday.
  3. The number is a measurement, not a report card. A higher reading says something about water and yesterday, not about your worth.
  4. Stay calm after holidays. The gain after Christmas or a party is partly water. Just carry on normally, no punishment.
  5. More than one measure. How do your trousers fit? How do you sleep? How often have you said no to the vending machine this year? That’s progress too.

Frequently asked questions

Is it bad to weigh yourself every day?

Not necessarily. In many studies, regular weighing is linked to more success losing weight and keeping it off. What matters is treating the number as a measurement and watching the trend over several weeks. If the scale weighs heavily on you, weigh yourself less often.

Why am I heavier on Mondays?

At weekends people often eat and drink differently, and the body holds more water and gut contents. Studies show weight is typically higher after the weekend and falls during the week.

What time of day should I weigh myself?

Ideally always under the same conditions: in the morning after getting up and using the toilet, before breakfast, in similar clothing and on the same scale.
