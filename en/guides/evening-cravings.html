How to Stop Evening Cravings: 7 Things That Really Help

Updated November 26, 2026 · ToughLove editorial team · about 5 min read

In short: Evening cravings usually have concrete triggers: eating too little or too irregularly during the day, tiredness, stress and fixed habits like snacking in front of the TV. If you eat enough during the day, change your triggers and give yourself a few minutes of distance in the moment, evenings get much easier.

Why cravings hit in the evening of all times

Several things come together at night: it’s been a long day, your energy is gone and nothing at home is keeping you busy. The most common causes:

  • Not eating enough during the day. If lunch is just a salad or you skip meals, you make up the energy in the evening, often with sweets and snacks.
  • Tiredness and lack of sleep. In a well-known study, after just two short nights, men had less of the satiety hormone leptin, more of the hunger hormone ghrelin and a clearly bigger appetite, especially for carb-rich foods (Spiegel et al., 2004).
  • Stress. Eating calms you for a while. After a hard day, reaching for chocolate is often a reward or an outlet, not hunger.
  • Habit. Sofa, series, crisps: once a routine has been repeated often enough, the situation alone triggers the urge.
  • Boredom. When there’s nothing left to do, eating becomes something to do.

Hunger or emotion? The quick check

Physical hunger builds slowly, a cheese sandwich or a soup would do, and it stops when you’re full. Emotional hunger arrives suddenly, wants something very specific (usually sweet, salty or fatty) and doesn’t stop when you’re full. Ask yourself: “Would I eat an apple right now?” If not, it’s probably not hunger.

7 strategies against evening cravings

  1. Eat enough during the day. Three proper meals with protein (yoghurt, eggs, legumes, fish) and fibre (vegetables, whole grains) keep you full longer than snacking.
  2. Set a dinner time. A proper dinner at a regular time removes the open-ended evening that invites grazing.
  3. The 10-minute rule. When the urge comes: a glass of water, a change of room, ten minutes of waiting. Cravings come in waves, and the wave is often much weaker after a few minutes.
  4. Rework your triggers. What you don’t see, you eat less often. Don’t keep snacks next to the sofa; put them in a cupboard you have to get up for.
  5. Take sleep seriously. If you regularly sleep too little, you’re fighting your own hormones at night. Going to bed earlier is sometimes the best weight loss strategy.
  6. Find another outlet for stress. Five minutes outside, a shower, a phone call or a minute of mindful breathing: when food is no longer your only outlet, it loses power.
  7. Allow instead of banning. Strict bans make the forbidden food even more tempting. If you want something sweet, put one portion on a plate, sit down and enjoy it. Not out of the packet.

What doesn’t help

Going hungry all day to “save calories for the evening” is one of the most common triggers for evening overeating. Blaming yourself after a slip doesn’t help either: guilt often leads straight to the next round.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I always get cravings at night?

Common reasons are eating too little or irregularly during the day, tiredness and lack of sleep, stress and fixed habits like snacking in front of the TV.

What helps right away against a craving?

Drink a glass of water, change rooms and wait about ten minutes. Cravings come in waves and often weaken after a few minutes.

Are evening cravings normal?

An occasional craving is normal. But if you often lose control and feel guilty afterwards, talk to your doctor or an eating disorder support service.
